Worn shock absorber signs: when to have the suspension checked

Bouncing, oil traces and uneven tyre wear deserve an assessment. One visible sign or a mileage figure is not the complete diagnosis.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Changes can develop gradually

Suspension behaviour can change slowly enough for the driver to become accustomed to it. Continued bouncing after an uneven surface or a less stable feel calls for professional inspection. It does not establish that only the shock absorber is responsible. Total mileage alone is also not sufficient reason to replace identical parts at the same moment on every vehicle. Operating conditions and the actual component condition need separate consideration.

Prepare observations safely

Report oil leakage, uneven tyre wear or recent impacts you have already noticed. Explain whether the change followed carrying a load, a repair, or no known event. Do not push the car from an unsafe position or dismantle mounts for a home assessment. Resistance to a single movement by hand does not measure the assembly's road performance. Photograph a visible concern only when the vehicle is safely parked, without climbing underneath it.

Understand the inspection result

The assessment should consider the whole assembly, including mounts and related components, rather than the shock absorber's appearance alone. Ask which findings are confirmed damage and which need further investigation. If replacement is proposed, clarify part selection, necessary associated work and manufacturer requirements. Whether adjustments are needed depends on the design, not a universal checklist. Ask how the recommended operations connect with the complaint that prompted the visit.

Safety and the agreed job

If the vehicle is difficult to keep on course, suddenly unstable or braking has deteriorated, stop safely and arrange assistance. Do not perform abrupt manoeuvres or hard braking to test the shock absorbers. Agree the available inspection and repair scope with CARBONLAB for the specific car. The estimate should distinguish resolving an established fault from desirable future maintenance. Judge the result against the original complaint rather than the fact that new parts were installed.
