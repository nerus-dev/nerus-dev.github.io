Coolant loss: what to observe and when to stop driving

Repeated coolant loss needs an explanation, not just another top-up. Record useful details and avoid the risks of a hot cooling system.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Compare levels under the same conditions

The reservoir level can differ between a cold and a hot engine. Follow the handbook's conditions for comparisons and record when a drop appears. A single casual observation does not establish major engine damage. However, repeated loss should not be treated as routine simply because adding more fluid temporarily restores the level.

Observations that help an investigation

Explain whether there are marks beneath the vehicle, an unusual cabin smell, changing temperature readings or recent cooling-system work. An external leak is one possibility, but the absence of an obvious puddle does not rule a problem out. Take photographs only from a safe distance, away from hot components and fans that may start automatically.

Do not open a hot cooling system

Never remove the cooling-system cap with the engine running or still hot: pressurised fluid and steam can escape. If the vehicle overheats or displays a dangerous temperature warning, stop safely, switch off and arrange assistance. Continuing to drive or repeatedly restarting as an experiment may worsen damage. A safe assessment must come before further use.

Questions before topping up or repair

Choose coolant by the applicable handbook specification, not colour alone. Mixing incompatible products can cause problems, and a sealing additive does not prove that a cause has been repaired. Ask which test located the loss, what parts are proposed and how the result will be checked. Confirm the selected workshop's available operations before arranging work.
