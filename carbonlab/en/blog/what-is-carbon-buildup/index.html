What is carbon buildup and why does it form?

Understanding deposits inside an engine.

Draft awaiting technical review before publication.

What we mean by carbon buildup

Carbon buildup is a general term for deposits that can form on engine parts. Their composition and location depend on engine design, operating conditions, crankcase ventilation and other factors.

Why GDI engines are discussed

In direct-injection engines, fuel is delivered straight into the cylinder. Petrol therefore does not wash the backs of the intake valves as it does in many port-injected engines. This is one factor in the tendency of some designs to accumulate deposits. Having GDI does not itself mean the valves need cleaning on a mandatory schedule.

Possible symptoms

Significant deposits can sometimes be associated with uneven running and a change in throttle response. These signs are not specific: ignition, fuel delivery, sensors and other components can produce similar behaviour.

What an owner can do

Start with a consultation about the engine model, mileage, service history and current concerns. Ask whether an inspection is needed, which cleaning approach is available and how the cost will be calculated.

Can a power increase be promised?

A fixed power increase after cleaning cannot be promised without measurements on that particular vehicle. The aim is to remove contamination where doing so is technically justified.

Uneven running and changes in response can have different causes. The need for work and its scope depend on an assessment of the particular vehicle.
