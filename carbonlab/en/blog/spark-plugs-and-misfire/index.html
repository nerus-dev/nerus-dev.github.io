Spark plugs and misfires: understanding rough engine operation

A worn plug is one possible cause of a misfire. Why shaking should not automatically lead to replacing all the spark plugs.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Combustion needs more than a spark

A petrol engine depends on ignition, a suitable mixture and sound mechanical conditions inside each cylinder. A combustion misfire may produce rough idle or hesitation while accelerating. Those symptoms do not identify the cause. A plug, coil or another component should be named as faulty only when an appropriate check supports that conclusion.

What plug inspection can reveal

Electrode wear, insulator damage and deposit appearance can help a technician investigate. A photograph of a removed plug cannot establish the engine's entire condition. The installed part number, replacement history and engine requirements also matter. A plug that works well in a friend's car is not necessarily suitable for your engine or its operating conditions.

Why new plugs may not fix it

When a different fault remains, replacing plugs alone may leave the same hesitation. Investigation should address the cause, rather than just visible deposits. Do not choose a gap, tightening specification or replacement interval from a universal internet figure. Use the vehicle and plug manufacturer's applicable guidance, and check whether the original complaint is resolved after the work.

When to stop driving

If the engine warning flashes alongside severe shaking or a sudden loss of power, stop safely, switch the engine off and arrange professional assistance. Do not load the engine to see whether the symptom will clear. Before consultation, record whether the concern happens cold, warm or under acceleration, and provide any known plug replacement history.
