Engine oil changes: what to check before choosing an oil

A viscosity label or familiar brand is not enough. Check engine requirements, applicable approvals and maintenance history.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Viscosity is only part of the specification

Oils with the same viscosity can carry different approvals and applications. Begin with the vehicle year, exact engine version and handbook requirements. Ask which requirement the selected product meets. A claim that an oil suits every engine does not replace checking the necessary approval. Previous oil colour or an engine's subjective sound cannot determine the correct specification.

Use the vehicle's maintenance requirements

There is no single mileage or number of months for every car. Manufacturer guidance, operating conditions and the service indicator must be considered together. When history is unknown, establish a clear starting record for future maintenance. Falling oil level and a scheduled change are separate concerns: topping up does not explain or repair an oil loss.

Keep a useful service record

Retain the oil name, approval, quantity, filter reference, date and odometer reading. Ask how the final level and absence of leaks were checked. Resetting a reminder does not, by itself, demonstrate the work completed. For later monitoring, measure the level under the conditions specified in the handbook so that comparisons are meaningful.

When not to wait for the next change

A red oil-pressure warning with the engine running is not a routine service reminder. Stop safely, switch off and do not continue hoping to reach the next planned change. Arrange assistance and follow your vehicle's instructions. Leakage or unusual consumption also needs investigation, rather than a speculative switch to a thicker oil.
