Car repair estimates: what the price should actually include

Different prices for the same complaint may cover different work. Compare the scope before accepting an estimate.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Connect the figure to a defined task

A general promise to fix the car does not describe the job. A useful estimate identifies the complaint, findings and proposed action. Distinguish an investigation charge from the cost of a selected repair. When the cause is not yet confirmed, a preliminary range should not be treated as a complete final price without understanding what could change it.

Compare parts and labour separately

Clarify part type, manufacturer or reference, quantity and whether it is new, reconditioned or used. Similar job names may cover different items. Ask about dismantling, consumables and necessary associated operations. A lower total may simply omit something another quotation already includes. Equivalent scope is needed for a meaningful comparison between prices.

Agree how extra findings are handled

Arrange to be contacted before additional problems lead to extra expenditure. Clarify who can authorise a change and how that agreement will be recorded. This is practical advice about organising work, not a statement of Armenian legal requirements. For an expensive or unclear proposal, a second professional opinion can help compare the reasoning rather than only the final numbers.

Keep the completion record

Retain the work list, installed-part details and payment document. Ask how resolution of the original complaint was checked and which conditions apply to the work. At CARBONLAB, cleaning cost depends on design and access; agree the scope and final price with the manager. Another vehicle's estimate is not a confirmed price for yours.
