Fuel smell in a car: when not to start the engine

Strong or recurring fuel odour is a safety concern. Control the risk before attempting to identify the source.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Control the immediate risk first

If there is a strong fuel smell inside or around the car, or a suspected leak, do not start or drive it. If already moving, stop safely and switch the engine off. Stay away from flames, smoking and actions that could create a spark. For fire or immediate danger, move to a safe place and call emergency services.

It is not necessarily the filler cap

Fuel odour can accompany a liquid leak, a vapour-system issue or fuel spilled while refuelling. Timing alone does not establish the source, even if the concern began after visiting a filling station. Tightening a cap does not prove there is no leak. Visible fluid combined with fuel odour calls for a safe inspection, not another start to test a theory.

Information for roadside assistance

Give the location, whether the engine was running, where the smell is noticeable and whether marks are visible on the ground from a safe position. Do not crawl underneath or disconnect pressurised fuel lines. Arrange towing to an appropriate workshop for a suspected leak. Driving there to avoid recovery costs creates an unnecessary risk to people and the vehicle.

Before the vehicle is restarted

Ask the technician to explain the source found, the corrective work and the check afterwards. A smell temporarily disappearing, or opening the windows, does not demonstrate that a fault has been repaired. Mention recent work if the odour followed it.
