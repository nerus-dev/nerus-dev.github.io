Engine using oil: leakage, burning or an inconsistent measurement?

Build a useful oil-consumption record before applying a universal limit or assuming that the engine needs an overhaul.

Images are illustrative and do not show CARBONLAB’s actual workshop or completed work.

Begin with a measurable record

Before judging oil consumption, record the level, quantity added and distance travelled. Measure under the handbook's conditions, including vehicle position and waiting time. Inconsistent checks on different days can create a false impression. There is no single acceptable figure for every engine: use the manufacturer's specification for the particular engine and operating conditions. A sudden change deserves attention even if the total mileage seems modest.

A falling level does not always mean burning

Oil can escape through an external leak or enter the combustion path. Assessment may include seals, crankcase ventilation, a turbocharger where fitted and the engine's internal condition. Mileage and a low level do not prove piston-ring damage. Separating the routes helps avoid a major repair when the source is elsewhere. Sometimes a sequence of checks and monitoring is needed rather than a decision from one inspection.

Bring information that can be compared

Provide the exact oil specification, change date or mileage, top-up history and whether smoke is present. Note marks on the parking area and whether smoke occurs at a cold start, during acceleration or continuously. Recent repairs matter too. An additive or thicker oil chosen without checking requirements may alter the symptom without correcting the cause. Keep receipts and consistent measurements so the assessment is based on a trend.

Oil level and oil pressure are different

For a red oil pressure warning, stop safely, switch off and do not continue the journey even if the level appears normal. Adding oil does not prove that pressure has recovered. Arrange assistance for a substantial leak, heavy smoke or a new knocking sound. During ordinary monitoring, avoid exceeding the maximum mark and use the specified oil. The absence of a puddle is not proof that continued driving is safe.
