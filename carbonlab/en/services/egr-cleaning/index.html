What to know

What to discuss with the manager

EGR returns a portion of exhaust gas to the intake under certain operating conditions. Deposits can form on the relevant parts. Cleaning does not resolve every EGR fault: electrical, mechanical and other causes are possible.

Uneven running and changes in response can have different causes. The need for work and its scope depend on an assessment of the particular vehicle.

EGR component cleaning and assessment of the underlying fault

The EGR system returns some exhaust gas to the intake under certain operating conditions. Deposits may form in relevant areas, but their effect and access to the parts depend on the engine and system design. Suitability for cleaning is assessed individually.

An EGR-related fault code does not establish that cleaning alone will resolve the problem. Causes can include an actuator, electrical circuit, sensor or mechanical fault. Symptoms and test results should be assessed before deciding which work on specific components is justified.

This service concerns cleaning the agreed EGR components with the necessary parts removed. It is different from throttle-body cleaning or cleaning the entire intake system. The parts covered, related operations and checks after reassembly should be clarified before work begins.

Frequently asked questions

Is EGR cleaning the same as throttle-body cleaning?

No. EGR is the exhaust-gas recirculation system; the throttle body is a different component. This service description does not include a confirmed offer of throttle-body cleaning. Agree the exact operations with the manager.

Will cleaning resolve every EGR fault?

No. If the cause involves an electrical circuit, sensor, actuator or damaged part, removing deposits may not resolve it. The decision should be based on test results.

Is the entire EGR system cleaned?

Not automatically. Access and appropriate scope vary by engine. The specific components, required removal and boundaries of the agreed work are established beforehand.
